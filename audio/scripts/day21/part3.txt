Which jobs are mine?.
Grandma was coming to see our new home, so Mum made a list of jobs. I thought I had to do everything on it. The list included cleaning the floor, washing up, making the guest bed and putting books on the shelf. I felt worried because I also had homework. Mum saw me staring at the list and explained that each person had a different job. I had to tidy my room and put a clean mug beside Grandma’s bed. Dad would clean the floor, and my brother would wash up. “You don’t have to move the furniture,” Mum added. “I will do that with Dad.” I finished my homework first and then started my two jobs. When Grandma arrived, she asked if she needed to bring in her bag herself. Dad offered to carry it. I showed her round and pointed out the guest room. She brought towels, but we explained that we had some ready for her. Later, I told Mum the list was easier when I understood my part. Next time, I would ask which jobs were mine instead of assuming every task was my responsibility. Helping at home meant sharing the work.
