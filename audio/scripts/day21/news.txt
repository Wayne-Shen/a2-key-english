A picture on a tea tin starts a journey.

When German student Levi Benjamin Petersen was in high school, a character on a tin of Chinese tea caught his attention. The character meant good fortune. He did not know this at first, but he wanted to understand its shape and the language behind it.

At that time, Petersen could not speak Chinese. His interest in one character became the start of a longer learning journey. Years later, he visited a tea garden in Fuzhou, in eastern China. There, local experts showed him how to pick and process tea.

He also learned a custom used to say thanks when someone pours tea. A few days later, he won the overall title in an international Chinese-language competition for college students in Xiamen. A small moment of curiosity had led him to new experiences.
