Bigger is not always better.
Our class opened a nature exhibition on Friday. We had three rooms, and each group wanted its room to be the most popular. My group made the sea room. Leo built a whale model that was bigger than all our other models. It was also the heaviest, so two adults helped us move it. I thought the biggest model would make our room the best. However, our first visitors stopped for longer in the garden room. That room had tiny beetles, spiders and nests. The pupils gave visitors magnifying glasses, and people enjoyed discovering small details. In the story room, younger children listened to a funny penguin story. It was not the longest story, but it made them laugh the most. We realized that size was not the only thing visitors liked. We added a question card beside our whale and invited people to guess how we had made it. Then they stayed to talk to us. At the end, our teacher did not choose one winning room. She asked us to name the most useful idea we had learned from another group. I chose the garden room’s questions. They helped visitors look carefully instead of just walking past.
