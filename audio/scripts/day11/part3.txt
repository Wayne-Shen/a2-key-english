Learning to ask the way.
Our class is visiting the town gallery today. We travel by bus and meet our teacher outside the station. She gives each pair a card with directions. My friend Eva carries the card, and I write notes with a pen. The first instruction says, “Walk along Market Street. Go past the grocery store and turn left at the corner.” We find the grocery store easily. Then Eva turns right instead of left. Soon we reach a restaurant, but the gallery is nowhere in sight. I want to hurry back, but Eva suggests asking someone. A member of the restaurant staff reads our card and shows us the mistake. “Go back to the corner,” she says. “Then walk across the square. The gallery is beside the library.” We thank her and follow the new directions. At the gallery, our teacher asks what helped us most. I say it was stopping to ask, rather than walking faster. Inside, we see a painting of the town as it looked eighty years ago. I recognise the square, but the old station is much smaller. On the bus home, Eva and I decide to make a better map for our next visit.
