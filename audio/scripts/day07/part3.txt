A useful present.
On Saturday I go shopping with my cousin Sara. I want a present for my brother, who likes drawing. I have eight pounds in my wallet, so I need to choose carefully. First we visit the toy department in a large store. There is a drawing set for twelve pounds. It has many colours, but it is too expensive for me. A smaller set costs five pounds. I nearly buy it, but Sara suggests looking in the bookshop next door first. The shop assistant shows us a book about drawing animals. Its usual price is ten pounds, but it is half-price today. I like the pictures because each one shows a simple step. Sara says that my brother can use the pencils he already has. I decide to buy the book. At the till I give the assistant eight pounds and receive three pounds back. I put the receipt in my wallet. We then visit the supermarket for a snack. I still have enough money for an apple. I am pleased because I find a useful present and stay within my budget.
