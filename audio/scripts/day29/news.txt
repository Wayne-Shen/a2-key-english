Sharing bee skills helps a community.

A women's group in Kenya has learned to keep bees and sell honey. The group has twenty-one members and works in Baringo County. Its leader, Grace Cheserem, remembers collecting their first honey in twenty twenty-two. Today, the women run a honey business together.

They learned from an agricultural worker called Aholipama Chelagat Rutto. She attended a training program in Changsha, China, in twenty seventeen. After returning to Kenya, she shared the skills with local women. The training included modern beehives, protective clothing and special tools.

In twenty twenty-five, the group collected six hundred and twelve kilograms of honey. Money from sales helps with family expenses and buying animals such as chickens and goats. Cheserem also describes sharing household responsibilities with her husband. The report shows knowledge traveling between countries and then being shared within a community.
