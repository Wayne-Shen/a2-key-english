A rare gibbon family welcomes a baby.

A rare gibbon family in Yunnan has a new baby. The animals live in the Wuliang Mountain National Nature Reserve. The healthy newborn has brought the family to nine members. A worker who watches the group says it is now the largest family in the area being monitored.

The mother holds her baby closely while she looks for food, rests and moves around. Other family members watch the area around them. The report gives a close view of how this animal family cares for its newest member.

Western black-crested gibbons are small apes native to Asia. They are critically endangered, which means they face a very high risk of disappearing. Local authorities estimate that about fifteen hundred remain worldwide. More than six hundred live on Wuliang Mountain. These are estimates, rather than exact counts of every animal.
