An invitation that welcomes everyone.
Our class wanted to welcome new pupils with a photo afternoon. Jo suggested that everyone bring a picture of something they liked. Ben, our new classmate, looked worried. He did not have a digital camera, and he had never helped with a school event before. “You can use mine,” I said. “I’ll bring it on Saturday.” Our teacher asked who would make the invitations. Eva offered to write them, while Leo said he would print them. Then we looked at the weather report. Rain was possible. “Shall we meet indoors?” Jo asked. We agreed to use the library instead of the garden. Before sending the invitations, Ben noticed that the start time was missing. He politely asked Eva to add it. She thanked him, and he began to look more confident. On Saturday, I showed Ben the camera controls. He chose to photograph a book that reminded him of his old school. We put the photos beside short notes explaining why each one mattered. At the end, Ben offered to help upload the display to the school website. I realized that an invitation worked best when it gave clear information and made people feel welcome. Offering practical help had made it easier for Ben to join in.
